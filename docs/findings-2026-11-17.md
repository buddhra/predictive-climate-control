# Findings, 2026-11-17: furnace timing, a first heat-gain figure, and a heat-budget fault

Follows `findings-2026-11-16.md`. One morning of data unless stated. Nothing here changes the model.

## The thermostat's heating signal was unreliable before 2026-11-16 5:44pm PST

`sensor.hvac_action` copies the `hvac_action` attribute of the Z-Wave thermostat. Until it was polled, the thermostat reported that value late and held it too long:

- Nov 15: one continuous "heating" report from 8:20am to 9:04am (44.6 minutes). The thermostat's half-degree temperature sensor shows two separate rises in that window, so the furnace heated twice and stopped in between.
- The report also started minutes after heat was called (target set at 8:12:00, "heating" first reported at 8:20:19).

**Fix in place:** an automation in Home Assistant (`automation.thermostat_refresh_operating_state`) asks the thermostat to refresh its values once a minute while it is in heat mode. The thermostat answers within a few seconds.

**Check, Nov 17 morning.** Four heating cycles, each compared with when heat was called and with the thermostat's temperature trace:

| Reported heating | Length | Heat called | Thermostat temperature peaked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6:46–7:01am | 15 min | 6:45:47 (window closed) | 7:02 |
| 7:16–7:24am | 8 min | 7:15:10 (target raised) | 7:26 |
| 7:46–7:53am | 7 min | 7:45:10 (target raised) | 7:54 |
| 8:16–8:22am | 6 min | 8:15:10 (target raised) | 8:23 |

Starts are caught within a minute. Stops land one to two minutes before the temperature peak.

**What this means for the backtests and the daily review:**

- `sensor.hvac_action` is trustworthy from 2026-11-16 5:44pm PST on. Before that, treat its start and stop times as approximate, and its durations as unknown.
- Warm air reaches the thermostat four to six minutes after heating starts. Rebuilding heating times from the temperature trace alone therefore undercounts each cycle by about that much.
- Nov 15 is the only earlier day affected (the furnace did not run from Nov 5 to Nov 14). Its heating total is uncertain: about 20 minutes in the morning, plus an unmeasured amount between roughly 12:25pm and 1:20pm. The "54 minutes" in `findings-2026-11.md` came from the unreliable signal.
- Nov 16: the review dropped hours 9 and 10 for a cycle reported at 9:51–10:07am. Heat was actually called at about 9:27am when the window closed. The same hours are dropped either way.

Nov 17, 6:45–8:30am. Outside held at 48 °F on the house sensor, the sun was barely up, and the bedroom was flat at 67.8 °F for 20 minutes beforehand. Bedroom values are 5-minute means.

| Heating | Bedroom before | Bedroom peak (time) | Rise | Per minute of heating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6:46–7:01 (15 min) | 67.80 | 68.90 (7:10) | +1.10 | 0.07 |
| 7:16–7:24 (8 min) | 68.86 | 69.44 (7:35) | +0.58 | 0.07 |
| 7:46–7:53 (7 min) | 69.30 | 69.98 (8:05) | +0.68 | 0.10 |

- The bedroom peaks about 10 minutes after heating stops, then gives back 0.1–0.2 °F over the next 10–15 minutes.
- Net over the morning: +2.0 °F from 6:50 to 8:15am on 30 minutes of heating, about 0.07 °F per minute.
- The thermostat's own reading (downstairs) rises about 2.5 °F in a cycle and drops 1 °F within 10 minutes of it ending. It is reading supply air and should not be used for this.

Limits: one morning, three cycles. The first two started within 30 minutes of the bedroom window closing, so only the third had an hour of settled conditions before it. The fourth cycle was still rising when the data was pulled. No figure yet for a warm afternoon, a cold night, or a long cycle. The bedroom sensor also shows a late-morning jump with the furnace off on a clear day (Nov 16, +1.2 °F between 10:40 and 10:50am), so cycles at that time of day should not be used.

This is the input a furnace term in the model would need. It is not enough to add one yet.

## Heat-budget fault: the target flips between 70 and 71 °F every 15 minutes

On Nov 17 the thermostat target went to 71 °F at 7:15, 7:45, and 8:15am and back to 70 °F at 7:00, 7:30, 8:00, and 8:30am. Each rise to 71 °F started a heating cycle, so the furnace ran once every 30 minutes. The 8:15 run sent a "Heat on to 71°F" notification, and that branch sends one every time it runs.

Cause, from the automation traces at 8:15 and 8:30am (the closed forecast said 65.9–66.0 °F at 9pm, peak 69.4–69.5 °F, so the budget wanted heat throughout):

1. At 8:15 the heat-budget branch of `Thermostat Control` ran and set 71 °F.
2. At 8:30 the same branch was skipped by its own "already at this target" check, which is correct: there was nothing to do.
3. Because that branch was skipped, the `choose` fell through to the older "home before 7pm" branch, which set the target back to the home setpoint, 70 °F.
4. At the next 15-minute forecast update the target is 70 °F again, so the heat-budget branch runs again. That matches the rises at 7:15, 7:45, and 8:15.

Fix (applied 2026-11-17 9:21am PST): the older branch no longer runs while the heat budget is in control. One condition was added to it:

```
{{ not ((budget_on or budget_heating) and not budget_met) }}
```

Tested against live values at 8:43am: it evaluates true, so the older branch would have been blocked.

**The heat budget is paused as of the same change.** With the fault fixed, the budget would hold 71 °F until the closed forecast reaches 70 °F at 9pm or peaks at 71 °F. The legacy forecast runs 2–4 °F cool for the evening (`findings-2026-11.md`), so that would mean more heating than the house needs; the flipping had been limiting the budget by accident. The owner chose to fix the fault and pause the budget until the new forecast is cut over.

- A toggle, `input_boolean.heat_budget`, now gates the budget. It is **off**. `budget_on` and `budget_heating` in `Thermostat Control` are both false while it is off.
- While paused: with the house closed and someone home before 7pm, heat holds the home setpoint (70 °F) when it is under 62 °F outside, and the 68 °F downstairs floor still applies above that.
- **At cut-over (plan step 3), decide whether to turn the toggle back on.** Turning it on needs nothing else; the automation re-evaluates when the toggle changes.
- Checked after the change: the target went to 70 °F and stayed there through the 9:30am forecast update.
